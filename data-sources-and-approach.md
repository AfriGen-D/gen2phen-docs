## Data sources and approach
AG2P was developed through a multi-layered data integration and curation process that combines information from:

- public genomic and pharmacogenomic databases (e.g. ClinVar, dbSNP, PharmGKB, AGMP, AGVD);
- African genomics initiatives (such as H3Africa and AGenDA) and regional population studies;
- peer-reviewed publications reporting variant–phenotype or variant–disease associations in African or Afro-descendant populations.

Data are manually curated by domain experts to ensure consistency, accuracy and contextual relevance. Curators review variant annotations, study metadata and population descriptors, applying standardised terminology aligned with the African Population Ontology (AfPO; https://github.com/h3abionet/afpo and https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/ontologies/afpo/) and other biomedical ontologies.

Manual curation of cardiovascular-related phenotypes and variant associations in African populations has been carried out using multiple data sources: **H3Africa publications, PharmGKB, ClinVar, GWAS Catalog, DisGeNET, ClinGen**, the **H3Africa Catalogue** and **PubMed**.

To improve efficiency and scalability, AG2P is also introducing **AI-assisted curation tools** (see Section 3.2.4) such as **natural language processing (NLP)** and **machine-learning-based algorithms**. By integrating data from diverse biomedical sources, AG2P bridges the gap between research discovery and clinical insight, empowering scientists and healthcare professionals to make informed, ancestry-aware decisions.
