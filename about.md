## Background and rationale
Despite major advances in genomics, Africa remains underrepresented in global genotype–phenotype databases, and no repository is dedicated to African data. This limits our ability to interpret genetic findings in an ancestry-aware manner and to translate them into meaningful clinical or public health insights. Understanding the complex relationship between genetic variation and phenotypic features is crucial for improving health outcomes. While much work has been conducted in European populations, more studies are needed in Africa to capture its genetic diversity.
In this context, and under the umbrella of AfriGenD, an NIH-funded initiative that supports research and the implementation of genomic medicine in Africa, the African Genotype-to-Phenotype (AG2P) project was established. AG2P systematically documents, harmonises and integrates genomic and phenotypic data relating to African individuals and cohorts within a curated, structured database.
The AG2P Database is a curated and structured knowledge base designed to uncover the links between genetic variation and phenotypic diversity (pathogenic or not) across African populations. It connects genetic variants to their associated genes, studies, diseases, phenotypes and clinical significance, creating a space to explore the genetic and phenotypic landscape of African populations.
